## Start with the meal you actually eat

A useful diary begins with the food on your table. If lunch is rice, ulam and vegetables, that is the meal to log. There
is no need to translate it into an unfamiliar bowl or remove the rice to make the interface happy. Busog keeps those
pieces visible as separate entries, so you can change one portion without rebuilding everything.

## A cup is a starting point

The cooked white rice record in the bundled USDA snapshot lists a cup at 158 grams. Busog uses that published weight for
its counter. Household cups, scooping styles and rice varieties vary, so a food scale can give you a better match for
the portion you are holding. The gram picker is always there when the default cup is not yours.

## Look at the whole plate

The diary adds the actual source values for rice, the chosen dish and any sides. It does not decide whether your lunch
was good or bad. It gives you a record you can review. A smaller or larger portion changes that record; it does not
change the value of the meal or the person eating it.

## Try it at the counter

Open Turo-turo, choose one dish and one cup of rice, then compare half and extra portions. Open the dish detail to see
the ingredient weights. This is a transparent model of a meal, not a measurement of every carinderia’s recipe.

Open [Busog](/busog/app/) and the [food library](/busog/library/). Ingredient references:
[USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/).
