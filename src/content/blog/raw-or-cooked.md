## Match the food to the scale

A gram weight only makes sense beside the right food record. Raw and cooked entries describe different states. Rice
takes up water when it cooks; a drained vegetable has already lost some water. Use the USDA description to check the
state before you apply a weight.

## Read past the first word

Search results can look similar at a glance. The useful words may be at the end: boiled, drained, roasted, with salt,
without salt. Busog preserves the original description and FDC ID even when it shows a familiar Filipino display name
first. That makes the selection open to inspection.

## Recipe components stay explicit

Busog recipes use the ingredient states written in the snapshot. The chicken composition uses cooked stewed breast; the
rice uses a cooked record. The instructions tell you to begin with those components. No hidden cooking-yield percentage
converts raw weights into cooked portions.

## When your meal differs

Choose a closer record or adjust the composition rather than treating a familiar dish name as proof of a match. Food
records are useful references. They cannot describe every variety, cooking method and portion on their own.
