## Build from components

A repeatable lunch can be a few cooked ingredients rather than a complicated recipe. Busog’s manok baon bowl combines a
defined chicken component, rice, greens and a measured seasoning. It is a composition you can inspect and adapt to your
own cooking.

## Weigh the batch consistently

Use the food states listed by the recipe. If you are dividing a batch into several boxes, keep track of the ingredients
that went into the whole batch and how you divide them. Equal serving counts assume equal shares. A visibly different
box deserves its own portion adjustment.

## Let the diary stay editable

Log the recipe serving when you eat it, then adjust the grams if your portion differs. The diary stores the food and
weight together. It is a record you can correct, not a promise that every prepared container must be identical.

## Follow food-safety guidance separately

The nutrition demo is not a storage or reheating guide. Follow local food-safety advice for cooling, refrigeration and
reheating. The recipe flow simply keeps the nutritional ingredients legible while you plan a familiar meal.
