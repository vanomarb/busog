## Begin with an ingredient list

A dish name is a useful shortcut, but it is not nutrition data. Busog’s ginisang munggo composition names its mung
beans, greens, tomato, onion and oil. Each ingredient has a USDA record and a gram weight. The total is the result of
those choices.

## Scale, then add

For each ingredient, divide its grams by 100 and multiply by the published per-100g value. Repeat for calories, protein,
carbohydrate and fat. Add the ingredient results. Divide the batch by its declared serving count. Busog keeps the
unrounded values during these steps and rounds only what it displays.

## Keep the assumptions visible

The full measured oil and sauce stay in this composition. If some is left in a pan, the plated result differs. If your
cook uses pork, coconut milk or a different amount of oil, that is another recipe. A transparent total is still a model
with a defined ingredient list.

## Inspect before accepting

The How we counted this panel exposes the calculation and source links. It gives you a way to judge a dish against your
own meal. The goal is a traceable starting point, not a claim of laboratory accuracy.
