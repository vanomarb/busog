## Pleasantly full.

Busog is Filipino for pleasantly full. It is the feeling after a meal that fits the moment, whatever is on the plate.
That feeling is where Busog starts.

Most nutrition apps ask you to understand their database before they understand your lunch. Busog starts at the counter
instead: point at what you are eating, and the numbers follow.

## Familiar does not mean identical.

A bolognese changes from kitchen to kitchen, and so does an adobo. So every dish in Busog is an explicit ingredient
list, with weights, USDA source records and the math open for you to check. You decide whether it matches your plate,
and you adjust the grams when it doesn't.

## Three ways to reach the same plate.

The dish counter, photo logging and USDA search or barcode lookup all land in the same diary. A small mark shows where
each entry came from, and every path uses the same numbers.

## Made for the way you eat.

Busog works for pasta, tacos, oatmeal and salmon bowls, and for rice, ulam and merienda. Switch the region in settings
and the food names, meals and counter dishes follow.
