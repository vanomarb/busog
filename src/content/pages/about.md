## Pleasantly full.

Busog is Filipino for pleasantly full. It is the feeling after a meal that fits the moment: rice on the plate, a
familiar ulam, something shared. That feeling is the starting point for this product concept.

Nutrition tools often ask you to understand their database before they understand your lunch. Busog explores a different
entrance: a carinderia counter you can point at. The numbers remain visible, but the meal comes first.

## Familiar does not mean identical.

Adobo changes from kitchen to kitchen. A cup changes from household to household. This demo does not pretend otherwise.
Each dish is one explicit ingredient composition, with weights, USDA source records and calculations open to inspection.
You can decide whether that composition resembles your meal.

## Three ways to reach the same plate.

Turo-turo, a simulated sample-photo review, and USDA search or packaged-food lookup all reach one typed diary. A source
label stays with each entry. There is no separate set of numbers for a more impressive-looking logging path.

## A working proposition.

Busog is a portfolio concept. The diary and calculations work; recognition and coach replies are simulations. There are
no real users represented by the mock history, no claimed results and no business partnerships implied. The open
question is practical: can a food tool feel more at home at a Filipino table?
